# Problem Validation

## Problem

People in crowded physical environments often cannot know whether a suitable space is available until they physically check the location.

This creates:

- Wasted walking time
- Uncertainty before reaching a location
- Difficulty finding multiple spaces together
- Repeated checking across rooms or areas
- Poor visibility into crowd levels

## Target users

### Primary
Students and visitors searching for an available or suitable space.

### Secondary
Facility operators who need better visibility into how areas are being used.

### Future
Organizations managing larger physical environments.

## Validation questions

The initial research should validate:

- How often users search for available space
- Which locations create the most friction
- How much time is spent searching
- Whether group seating matters
- Whether crowd information changes decisions
- Whether users trust camera-derived occupancy information
- Which privacy concerns are most important

## Success signals

The problem is worth solving if users consistently report:

- Frequent availability uncertainty
- Repeated location checking
- Meaningful time lost searching
- Strong interest in real-time availability
- Strong interest in group-space discovery
- Acceptance of privacy-preserving occupancy detection

## Validation method

Use an anonymous survey and controlled prototype testing before scaling the system.

The product should be built around demonstrated user pain, not assumptions.
